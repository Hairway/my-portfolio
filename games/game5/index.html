Concept · Spec · Full production

Turn the game's own sorting loop into an instrument. Every arrow fires a short beat when it is tapped, so taking the picture apart writes a track instead of just clearing a board — the player is the one playing the music, which is what makes the loop worth watching.

Picture and board
  • The level subject is a boombox, and the sound design follows the subject — this level is hip-hop and funk.
  • Arrow tiles are assembled into the silhouette of that picture, so the figure is recognisable before a single tap.
  • Each direction owns its own colour as well as its own sound, so the four types are distinguishable with the volume off.
  • Three lives sit at the top of the screen from the first frame.
  • The background stays neutral but leans on colour and pattern that read as musical.
Scenes
Scene 1
Reveal
  • The boombox picture holds full screen for two to three seconds — long enough to register, short enough not to cost the hook.
  • It then dissolves and arrow tiles take its place, still holding the boombox shape.
  • Tap to play comes up over the board.
  • The three lives are visible from the start, so the stakes are set before the first move.
Scene 2
Zoom and teach
  • The camera pushes into one part of the figure so a single arrow is unmistakable.
  • The tutorial finger points at it and taps.
  • Tapping up fires beat type 1 and the arrow flies off along its own direction — the direction of travel and the sound arrive together.
Tutorial · finger points at the first arrow
Scene 3
Play the beat
  • Every direction owns a unique short beat, and the four have to mix with each other rather than merely differ.
  • Tapping left fires beat type 2, and so on for each direction.
  • Each arrow leaves a musical trail behind it — sound waves like a speaker cone, or notes flying off.
  • A light neutral loop runs underneath the whole level so the player's taps land on something rather than in silence.
  • The player takes the figure apart at their own tempo, and the melody is whatever order they choose.
Scene 4
Mistakes cost
  • An arrow sent into another arrow is a miss — the rule is spatial, so it is learned by doing rather than by text.
  • The screen flashes red briefly and one life is removed.
  • The miss has its own reversed beat, so an error is audible before it is read.
  • Three misses end the run.
Scene 5
Clear the figure
  • The player takes the whole figure apart, playing the melody as they go.
  • After four seconds of inactivity the tutorial finger comes back on the next legal arrow.
  • The sounds have to be mixed pleasantly — this is the one place where audio is the mechanic, not decoration.
End states
Win
Figure cleared
  • YOU BEAT THIS! on a red ground — the pun is the point.
  • The CTA underneath reads Choose another level.
  • Two next-level icons sit below it: a microphone and a synthesiser.
  • The finger moves between the two icons rather than pointing at one, so the choice reads as a choice.
Fail
Out of lives
  • Three misses end the run.
  • Out of lives with a heart icon and a Restart button, on the same red ground as the win card.
  • A tap takes the player to the store.
