Railroad Empire

Concept · Spec · Full production

Put a resource-extraction and territory-upgrade loop — the genre that was working everywhere else — inside a train strategy. The emphasis is on mining the resource, hauling it and upgrading: the player holds the throttle themselves, and every delivery pays for the next building.

Location
  • Forest and a lake with the railway laid as a closed ring around it, so the player passes every building on every lap.
  • The depot sits at the bottom edge of the frame — start and finish of every run.
  • Buildings are placed around the ring: the sawmill first, then the stone mine, then the iron mine, each in its own zone beside the track.
  • The layout comes from earlier playables in the series, with the buildings moved so the route reads as a progression rather than a lap.
Scenes
Scene 1
Opening
  • The camera shows the circular location in full, so the shape of the route is understood before the player is asked for anything.
  • The mentor character appears on the left with a speech bubble whenever a prompt is due.
  • The coin bar opens at 200 — exactly the price of the sawmill, so the first purchase is affordable and obvious.
  • The sawmill card pulses during the tutorial.
  • Movement is a held press, not a tap: HOLD TO MOVE.
Tutorial · finger does a short tap and hold — HOLD TO MOVE
Scene 2
Build the sawmill
  • The first building costs 200 coins and is bought from a card with a plank icon on it.
  • Coins fly out of the coin bar into the card, and the sawmill spawns with a flash, a puff of wood dust and then a constant light smoke.
  • The building is the first thing the player owns, so the economy starts from their decision.
Tutorial · Build the sawmill
Scene 3
Load and deliver
  • A long dashed loading zone sits beside the sawmill with a plank icon inside it.
  • Stopping in the zone fills that icon from the bottom up; each fill loads one wagon.
  • Wagons are open on purpose — a loaded wagon visibly carries planks, stone or iron, so cargo needs no UI.
  • Driving back to the depot sells the load: 10 planks pay 50 coins, so three wagons of wood come to 150.
  • The zone turns slightly green on entry with a confirming sound, and a rising hum plays while the plate fills.
Tutorial · Stop at the sawmill to load the planks
Scene 4
Upgrade the train
  • The upgrade panel opens on the depot with two cards: an extra wagon and more speed.
  • Both double in price with every level — wagons run 100, 200, 400, 800 and speed runs 75, 150, 300, 600.
  • The fourth level of either upgrade is the exit: tapping it sends the player to the store.
  • A third card, a new locomotive at 600, joins the panel later in the run.
Tutorial · Buy an upgrade
Scene 5
New mines
  • New zones open on the track and are bought with resources rather than coins: the stone mine costs 40 wood, the iron mine 60 wood and 60 stone.
  • A long resource bar appears on the depot with sections for wood, stone and iron, showing the total stock.
  • Better resources pay better — 10 stone is 75 coins, 10 iron is 100 — so the route the player drives gets more valuable as it gets longer.
  • Each mine gets its own working sound: saws for wood, drills for stone, picks for iron.
End state
Win
Any one of three
  • The playable ends when the player builds the gold ingot factory, buys any fourth-level train upgrade, or completes fifteen laps.
  • The three conditions exist so the run ends on whichever way the player chose to play it.
  • Any tap at that moment goes to the store.
